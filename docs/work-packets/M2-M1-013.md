## Follow-up checkpoint — mobile/control refinement
The private live surface received a bounded customer-directed refinement without expanding the public data surface:

- Dashboard consumption was reduced to a true narrow two-column phone layout with materially larger text and only executive information first; detailed audit information remains on supporting tabs.
- The sign convention is now explicit and invariant: positive means behind, negative means ahead, zero means on target. Baseline-day actual status is on-track rather than degraded by pre-baseline history or configuration warnings.
- User-editable target weeks, operating-reserve months, debt APRs, known one-time commitment amount, current/temporary retirement contribution rates, and desired signed forecast buffer recalculate dependent Sheet formulas immediately. Provider refresh is required only for provider-owned observations, not for assumption recalculation.
- A known one-time commitment can be edited in the Dashboard, feeds committed cash/runway and forecast burden, and is designed to be cleared only after matching posted evidence prevents double counting.
- The budget source and normalized-actual-spend source are projected separately rather than blended into one misleading forecast.
- The retirement scenario now operates on contribution rates and a configurable cash-realization assumption. The guaranteed-match floor is evidence-backed by current plan terms; the second driver's current election remains an explicit private assumption until independently verified.
- A desired signed forecast buffer allows the household to model exactly-on-target or intentionally-ahead outcomes. The estimated temporary-contribution duration responds dynamically to target, mileage, spending, APR, commitment and contribution-rate inputs.
- AM/PM brief definitions were updated in place to use the corrected sign convention, aggregate workweek mileage only, dual forecast bases, committed-cash semantics, Day-0 status semantics and the rate-based retirement scenario.

## Follow-up checkpoint — three-axis spending review and telemetry
The private live Financial Escape surface now preserves spending intent on three independent user-controlled axes instead of collapsing behavior into one category:

- **Purpose category** records what the economic spend was for.
- **Necessity** is independently selectable as `NECESSARY`, `UNNECESSARY`, or `UNKNOWN`.
- **Allowance** is independently selectable as `YES`, `NO`, or `UNKNOWN`; it means the spend was deliberately permitted, not merely that it counts against an escape-control budget.
- Positive economic-spend rows require category, necessity, allowance, and vendor resolution before becoming fully reviewed. Credits, transfers, card payments, and other non-spend rows remain explicitly non-spend instead of receiving fabricated lifestyle labels.
- Unknown/unreviewed historical rows remain unknown until classified by the user; MIRA does not retroactively guess necessity or allowance state.
- A dedicated private Spending Telemetry projection separates necessary vs unnecessary spend, allowance vs non-allowance spend, all four necessity/allowance intersections, review completeness, transaction counts, ticket size, monthly pivots, category pivots, top allowance-bending vendors, and recent detail views.
- `UNNECESSARY + YES` means deliberately permitted discretionary spend. `UNNECESSARY + NO` is the explicit allowance-bending / unplanned-discretionary signal.
- The review and telemetry remain a private projection over stable transaction/event identity; this follow-up does not make the spreadsheet a second financial authority or expose private transaction values in public Git.
